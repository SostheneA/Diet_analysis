We assessed the robustness of within- versus between-period differences in diagnostic-family frequencies using 2000 nonparametric bootstrap replicates at each spatial resolution (gulf-wide, ecoregion and stratum), where data were available. Species-by-size-class cells were resampled with replacement within each fixed spatial unit. The same cell multiplicities were used across scenarios, thresholds and biomass/occurrence modes within each replicate. Family frequencies were calculated among classifiable cells, explicitly retaining zero frequencies for absent families. For each scenario, spatial-unit percentages were averaged with equal weights at each threshold and then averaged across thresholds. Within- and between-period scenarios were subsequently averaged separately with equal scenario weights. Contrasts were defined as within minus between for Stability and between minus within for Substitution, in percentage points. Pointwise 95% percentile bootstrap intervals were calculated directly for these contrasts. Intervals entirely above zero supported the prespecified direction. These intervals are conditional on the original classifications and classifiability; raw stomach observations and the original statistical tests were not resampled or rerun. Spatial units were held fixed and dependence between distinct cells was not explicitly modelled. Individual-unit and scenario-pair contrasts were treated as exploratory; intervals were not multiplicity-adjusted. Contrasts with missing required scenarios or insufficient valid bootstrap replicates were not interpreted.
